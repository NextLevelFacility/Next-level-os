# Retention Playbook

> [!quote] It's far cheaper to keep a member than to win a new one — and long-term members are the ones who refer. Retention is where the real money and the real mission live. A leaky bucket can't scale.

## Why members leave (and how we prevent it)
| They leave because... | We prevent it by... |
|---|---|
| They stopped seeing progress | Visible re-testing & progress shown ([[Coaching & Training Delivery]]) |
| They felt like a number | [[Culture Code|Belonging]] + known by name ([[Member Experience & Front Desk]]) |
| Life/schedule got hard | Flexibility, pauses, proactive outreach |
| A bad experience went unfixed | Fast [[Member Experience & Front Desk|complaint recovery]] |
| They drifted / lost the habit | Early at-risk detection (below) |

## The retention system

### 1. Deliver results people can see
The #1 retention driver. Re-test, track, and **show** progress to athlete + parent regularly. Nobody quits when they're visibly winning.

### 2. Build belonging
Names, relationships, culture, community ([[Culture Code]]). Members stay for people as much as for gains.

### 3. Proactive parent communication
For youth athletes, retaining the *parent* is retaining the member. Regular progress updates = trust = renewal.

### 4. Early at-risk detection
> [!warning] Watch for the signals
> - Attendance drops off pace
> - Went quiet / stopped engaging
> - Missed a payment ([[Finance & Admin|dunning]])
> - Complained and it wasn't fully closed
>
> **Trigger:** any signal → a real human reaches out *before* they cancel.

### 5. The cancellation conversation
- Understand the *real* reason (not the polite one).
- Offer the right save: pause, adjust, re-motivate — or let go gracefully.
- **Always log the reason.** Patterns tell you what to fix.

### 6. Win-backs
- Stay in touch with good members who left.
- Re-invite at natural moments (new season, new program, a win they'd love).

## The metric
Track **churn %** and **average member lifetime** in [[Key Metrics]]. Small churn improvements compound massively — a 2-point drop can be worth more than a marketing campaign.
